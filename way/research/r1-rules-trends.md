## A · Apple: App Store Review Guidelines and related Apple requirements

**R1 · Data from health contexts (HealthKit and others) may not be used for advertising, marketing or data mining. The app must disclose the specific health data it collects, must not write false data into HealthKit, and must not store personal health information in iCloud.** Guideline 5.1.3(i)–(ii). `opened`
- https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ · 2026-10-01 · 5.1.3(i): "Apps may not use or disclose to third parties data gathered in the health, fitness, and medical research context—including from … HealthKit API … for advertising, marketing, or other use-based data mining purposes other than improving health management, or for the purpose of health research, and then only with permission. … You must disclose the specific health data that you are collecting from the device." 5.1.3(ii): "Apps must not write false or inaccurate data into HealthKit … and may not store personal health information in iCloud."

**R2 · Sending personal data to a third-party AI (for us, Gemini on Google Cloud) needs clear disclosure and explicit permission first.** Guideline 5.1.2(i), clarified on 13 Nov 2025. The same guideline bars making system functions a condition of using the app. `opened`
- https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ · 2026-10-01 · 5.1.2(i): "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so. … Your app may not require users to enable system functionalities (e.g. push notifications, location services, tracking) in order to access functionality".
- https://developer.apple.com/news/ (post "Updated App Review Guidelines now available", 13 Nov 2025) · 2026-10-01 · "5.1.2(i): Clarifies that you must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so."

**R3 · Consent: the app must ask for consent to collect data, make withdrawing consent easy, and never make paid features depend on granting data access. It must ask only for data relevant to its core function.** Guidelines 5.1.1(ii)–(iv). `opened`
- https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ · 2026-10-01 · 5.1.1(ii): "Paid functionality must not be dependent on or require a user to grant access to this data. Apps must also provide the customer with an easily accessible and understandable way to withdraw consent." 5.1.1(iii): "use the out-of-process picker or a share sheet rather than requesting full access to protected resources like Photos". 5.1.1(iv): "Where possible, provide alternative solutions for users who don't grant consent."

**R4 · Account deletion has to be available inside the app. It must delete the whole account record and its personal data, and deactivation alone does not count. Apps outside regulated industries must not route deletion through a phone call or email. Sign in with Apple tokens must be revoked. Users with subscriptions must be told that Apple keeps billing them.** Guideline 5.1.1(v) and Apple's deletion guidance. `opened`
- https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ · 2026-10-01 · 5.1.1(v): "If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app."
- https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/ · 2026-10-01 · "Offer to delete the entire account record, along with associated personal data. … only offering to temporarily deactivate or disable an account is insufficient." · "Apps not operating in highly regulated industries should not require people to make a phone call, send an email, or go through other support flows." · "Apps that support Sign in with Apple should use the Sign in with Apple REST API to revoke user tokens." · "If the user has auto-renewable subscriptions, notify them that their billing will continue through Apple".

**R5 · The privacy policy must be linked both in App Store Connect and inside the app. It must list every use of the data, say how long data is kept and how deletion works, and say how to revoke consent. It must also confirm that third parties protect the data equally.** Guideline 5.1.1(i). `opened`
- https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ · 2026-10-01 · "Explain its data retention/deletion policies and describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion of the user's data." · "Confirm that any third party with whom an app shares user data … will provide the same or equal protection of user data".

**R6 · Health accuracy claims need the data and method disclosed, or Apple rejects the app. Medical apps get closer review and should tell users to check with a doctor.** Guideline 1.4.1. `opened`
- https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ · 2026-10-01 · "Medical apps that could provide inaccurate data or information, or that could be used for diagnosing or treating patients may be reviewed with greater scrutiny. Apps must clearly disclose data and methodology to support accuracy claims relating to health measurements, and if the level of accuracy or methodology cannot be validated, we will reject your app." · "Apps should remind users to check with a doctor in addition to using the app and before making medical decisions."

**R7 · HealthKit may be used only for health and fitness, and the app description must say it integrates with Health. HealthKit data may not be used for ads or sold. It may go to a third party only with express permission, and only to a party that itself provides a health or fitness service to the user. The app cannot tell when the user has denied read access.** Guideline 2.5.1 and Apple's HealthKit privacy documentation. `opened`
- https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ · 2026-10-01 · 2.5.1: "Apps should use APIs and frameworks for their intended purposes and indicate that integration in their app description. … HealthKit should be used for health and fitness purposes and integrate with the Health app."
- https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy (read as `…/tutorials/data/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy.json`) · 2026-10-01 · "You must not disclose any information gained through HealthKit to a third party without express permission from the user. Even with permission, you can only share information to a third party if they also provide a health or fitness service to the user." · "an app isn't aware when the user denies permission to read data. From the app's point of view, no data of that type exists." · "You must also provide a privacy policy for any app that uses the HealthKit framework."

**R8 · Data from HealthKit and from the camera and photo APIs may not be used for marketing, advertising or data mining, including by third parties.** Guideline 5.1.2(vi). This covers meal photos. `opened`
- https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ · 2026-10-01 · "Data gathered from the HomeKit API, HealthKit, … or from depth and/or facial mapping tools (e.g. ARKit, Camera APIs, or Photo APIs) may not be used for marketing, advertising or use-based data mining, including by third parties."

**R9 · A paid AI tier has to use in-app purchase. An auto-renewing subscription must give ongoing value and last at least 7 days, and the app must say what the user gets before asking them to subscribe. Purchased credits may not expire. The guideline text I fetched has no AI-specific payment rule.** Guidelines 3.1.1 and 3.1.2(a), (c). `opened`
- https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ · 2026-10-01 · 3.1.1: "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app, (by way of example: subscriptions …), you must use in-app purchase." · "Any credits or in-game currencies purchased via in-app purchase may not expire". 3.1.2(a): "you must provide ongoing value to the customer, and the subscription period must last at least seven days and be available across all of the user's devices." · examples include "software as a service ("SAAS"); and cloud support." 3.1.2(c): "Before asking a customer to subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price."

**R10 · An app that needs sensitive user information should be submitted by a legal entity, not an individual developer.** Guideline 5.1.1(ix). `opened`
- https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ · 2026-10-01 · "Apps that provide services in highly regulated fields (such as … healthcare …) or that require sensitive user information should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer."

**R11 · The App Store privacy label has separate "Health" and "Fitness" data types and separate types for photos and audio. Data collected by third-party SDKs must be declared too.** `opened`
- https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/ · 2026-10-01 · Health: "Health and medical data, including but not limited to data from the Clinical Health Records API, HealthKit API … or any other user provided health or medical data". Fitness: "Fitness and exercise data". Audio Data: "The user's voice or sound recordings". "You need to identify all of the data you or your third-party partners collect, unless the data meets all of the criteria for optional disclosure."

**R12 · Required-reason APIs (UserDefaults, file timestamps, disk space, system boot time, active keyboard) must be declared with an approved reason in a privacy manifest. App Store Connect has rejected uploads without them since 1 May 2024. Each SDK must ship its own manifest.** `opened`
- https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/describing-use-of-required-reason-api (JSON form) · 2026-10-01 · "Starting May 1, 2024, apps that don't describe their use of required reason API in their privacy manifest file aren't accepted by App Store Connect." · "Your third-party SDK can't rely on the privacy manifest files for apps that link the third-party SDK". Manifest key: `NSPrivacyAccessedAPITypes`.

**R13 · Apple's age-rating questionnaire names "calorie tracking" and "dieting advice" as *Health or Wellness Topics*, and that content maps to **9+**. "Medical or Treatment Information" maps to 13+ if infrequent and 16+ if frequent. Apple's rating therefore does not by itself make the app adults-only.** `opened`
- https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/age-ratings-values-and-definitions · 2026-10-01 · "Health or Wellness Topics: Content that provides self-care or lifestyle recommendations. May include: calorie tracking, dieting advice, or exercise recommendations." The 9+ row lists "Medical or Wellness | Health and wellness topics". The 16+ row lists "Frequent medical or treatment information".

**R14 · New since 26 Mar 2026: an app in the Health & Fitness category that is distributed in the EEA, UK or US must declare in App Store Connect whether it is a regulated medical device. New apps must do this now, and existing apps by early 2027. An app that is not a device answers "No".** `opened`
- https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=nyqbfz1y (26 Mar 2026) · 2026-10-01 · "If you distribute in the EEA, UK, or U.S. and your app meets either of the following criteria, you'll need to provide a regulated medical device status in App Store Connect … Its primary or secondary category is Health & Fitness or Medical … If your app is not a regulated medical device, you can select No."

## C · Saudi Arabia: Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) and SDAIA regulations

The official SDAIA hosts were blocked. I opened English copies of SDAIA's documents in `nedalaltiti/agentic-rag` on GitHub, under `data/sample/`. Their filenames match SDAIA's download names, and each document carries SDAIA's own version and date.

**R20 · The PDPL also covers processing done outside the Kingdom of data about people living in it. Health data is defined broadly and counts as *Sensitive Data*.** Law Arts. 1–2. `opened (mirror)`
- https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nedalaltiti/agentic-rag/HEAD/data/sample/Personal+Data+English.md (Royal Decree M/19, amended by M/148, 27 Mar 2023; official host sdaia.gov.sa) · 2026-10-01 · Art. 2(1): "including the Processing of Personal Data related to individuals residing in the Kingdom by any means from any party outside the Kingdom." Art. 1(13): "Health Data: Any Personal Data related to an individual's health condition, whether their physical, mental or psychological conditions". Art. 1(11) lists "Health Data" under Sensitive Data.

**R21 · SDAIA's guide says inferences about health are sensitive data. Legitimate interest cannot be the legal basis for sensitive data, consent to it must be explicit, and it may never be used for marketing. The PDPL took effect on 14 Sep 2023, and the grace period ended on 14 Sep 2024.** `opened (mirror)` (SDAIA guide v1.0, Dec 2023)
- https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nedalaltiti/agentic-rag/HEAD/data/sample/Guide+to+the+saudi+PDP+law+for+controllersprocessors.md · 2026-10-01 · "The inferences that the pharmacy makes about the health conditions of their customers … will be considered sensitive data" · "If you rely on consent as a lawful basis for processing of sensitive data, such consent must be explicit" · "even with an individual's consent, you may not process sensitive data for marketing purposes" · "The PDPL came into force on 14 September 2023." · "compliance with the PDPL by 14 September 2024."

**R22 · Consent must be given freely by someone with full legal capacity. It must be documented so it can be proved later, and each purpose needs its own consent. It must be *explicit* for sensitive data and for decisions made solely by automated processing. Withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it.** Implementing Regulation Arts. 11–12. `opened (mirror)`
- https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nedalaltiti/agentic-rag/HEAD/data/sample/The+impelementing+regulation+of+the+personal+data+protection+law.md · 2026-10-01 · Art. 11(1): "c) Consent shall be given by a person who has full legal capacity. d) Consent shall be documented through means allowing future verification … e) A separate consent shall be obtained for each Processing purpose." Art. 11(2): "explicit … a) When the Processing involves Sensitive Data … c) When decisions are made solely based on automated Processing". Art. 12(2): "ensuring that consent withdrawal is as or easier than obtaining."

**R23 · On request, the controller must destroy the data. That includes telling everyone it shared the data with and destroying all copies, backups included. Rights requests must be answered within 30 days, extendable by 30 more.** Law Art. 4(5); Implementing Regulation Arts. 3 and 8. `opened (mirror)`
- (same Implementing Regulation copy) · 2026-10-01 · Art. 8(1): "The Controller shall destroy Personal Data … a) Upon Data Subject's request." Art. 8(2): "a) … notify other parties to whom the Controller has disclosed such Personal Data and request their Destruction … c) Destroy all copies of the Personal Data stored in the Controller's systems, including backups". Art. 3(a): "within a period not exceeding (30) days … extension period does not exceed an additional (30) days".

**R24 · Breaches must be reported to SDAIA within 72 hours. A written impact assessment is required for processing sensitive data, using new technologies or making automated decisions. Health data needs extra controls: separated duties, every processing stage documented, and processor contracts that pass the obligations on.** Implementing Regulation Arts. 24–26. `opened (mirror)`
- (same copy) · 2026-10-01 · Art. 24(1): "within a delay not exceeding (72) hours of becoming aware of the incident". Art. 25(1): "a) Processing of Sensitive Data … c) … Processing Personal Data based on newly adopted technologies, or making decisions based on automated Personal Data Processing." Art. 26(4): "Document all stages of Health Data Processing and provide the means to identify the person in charge for each stage." Art. 26(5): "The agreement between the Controller and the Processors … shall include provisions that oblige them to abide by the procedures and measures stated in this Article."

**R25 · A data protection officer (DPO) is required when the controller's core activity is processing sensitive data, or when it involves regular and systematic monitoring. SDAIA's own example of such monitoring is collecting health and fitness data from wearables.** SDAIA DPO rules v1.0, Aug 2024. `opened (mirror)`
- https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nedalaltiti/agentic-rag/HEAD/data/sample/Rules+for+Appointing+Personal+Data+Protection+Officer.md · 2026-10-01 · "3- Core activities of Controller are based on processing of sensitive Personal Data." · "examples of regular and systematic monitoring: 1- Collecting personal health and fitness data through wearable devices."

**R26 · Transfers outside the Kingdom are allowed for listed purposes, including "to provide a service or benefit to the data subject". They also need an adequate level of protection in the destination (SDAIA publishes the list of adequate countries) and must be limited to the minimum data. Exemptions based on safeguards exist (standard contractual clauses, binding corporate rules, certification), but the two that cover service delivery or research to certified recipients exclude sensitive data. A risk assessment is required before continuous or widespread transfers of sensitive data.** Law Art. 29; Transfer Regulation v2.0, Aug 2024. `opened (mirror)`
- https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nedalaltiti/agentic-rag/HEAD/data/sample/Regulation+on+Personal+Data+Transfer+Outside+the+Kingdom.md · 2026-10-01 · Art. 2: "2. To provide a service or benefit to the subject of the personal data." Art. 4(2)(D): "if the transfer or disclosure is to a party that has received an approval certificate … provided that the data must not be sensitive." Art. 7(1): "B. Transferring or disclosing sensitive data to entities outside the Kingdom on a continuous or widespread basis."
- (law copy, R20) · Art. 29(2): "B. There is an adequate level of protection for Personal Data outside the Kingdom … C. The Transfer or Disclosure shall be limited to the minimum amount of Personal Data needed."

**R27 · Penalties: disclosing sensitive data with intent to harm can bring up to 2 years in prison or a SAR 3 million fine. Other breaches can bring fines of up to SAR 5 million, doubled for repeat offences.** Law Arts. 35–36. `opened (mirror)`
- (law copy, R20) · 2026-10-01 · Art. 35(1): "imprisonment for a period not exceeding (two years), or a fine not exceeding (three million) Riyals". Art. 36(1): "a warning or a fine not exceeding (five million) Riyals".

## D · Egypt: Personal Data Protection Law 151 of 2020

**R28 · Egypt's law treats physical, mental and psychological health data as sensitive, and treats all children's data as sensitive. Processing any personal data needs explicit consent. Sensitive data needs a licence from the Personal Data Protection Centre plus written, explicit consent. Breaches must be reported within 72 hours. Transfers abroad need an adequate destination and a licence or permit from the Centre, with narrow exceptions that rest on explicit consent. Entities have one year from the executive regulations to comply.** `opened (mirror)`. This is an English rendering of the law in `Kiramido1/Amenly-Product`. The official Arabic text was not opened.
- https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Kiramido1/Amenly-Product/HEAD/clean%20data/clean%20data/processed/text/egypt_pdpl.json · 2026-10-01 · Definitions: "Sensitive Personal Data: Data that reveals mental, psychological, physical, or genetic health … In all cases, children's data shall be deemed sensitive personal data." Art. 2: "It is prohibited to collect, process, disclose, or reveal personal data by any means without the explicit consent of the data subject". Art. 12: "without a license from the Center … written and explicit consent must be obtained". Art. 7: "notify the Center within seventy-two (72) hours". Art. 14: "unless the destination provides a level of protection not less than that stipulated under this Law, and subject to licensing or authorization by the Center." Issuance Art. 6: "within one (1) year from the date of issuance of such Regulations."

**R29 · The executive regulations were issued by Decree No. 816 of 2025 on 1 Nov 2025 and took effect on 2 Nov 2025. ## E · GDPR, if EU residents can download the app

**R30 · The GDPR reaches a controller outside the EU that offers services to people in the EU. Such a controller must appoint an EU representative unless its processing is occasional and involves no large-scale special-category data.** Arts. 3(2) and 27. `opened (mirror)`
- (tsv above) · 2026-10-01 · Art. 3(2): "processing of personal data of data subjects who are in the Union by a controller or processor not established in the Union, where the processing activities are related to the offering of goods or services, irrespective of whether a payment of the data subject is required". **R31 · Health data is "special category" data and needs explicit consent (or another Art. 9(2) basis). Withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it. Erasure must happen without undue delay, and rights requests must be answered within one month. Large-scale special-category processing needs a data protection impact assessment (DPIA).** Arts. 4(15), 7(3), 9, 12(3), 17, 35(3)(b). `opened (mirror)`

Art. 7(3): "It shall be as easy to withdraw as to give consent." Art. 12(3): "within one month of receipt of the request". Art. 35(3)(b): "processing on a large scale of special categories of data".

## Implications for our map

- **Adults only, enforced in the app (R16, R22, R28, R13).** Google's generative AI terms forbid any service likely to be used by under-18s. Saudi consent requires full legal capacity, and Egypt treats children's data as sensitive. Apple's own questionnaire will rate a calorie tracker 9+, so the rating will not keep minors out. Onboarding therefore needs an age gate of 18+ before any cloud AI runs, and under-18s should get no account (open question: whether to use Apple's Declared Age Range API).
- **Proposed calorie-floor policy default for the nutrition approver to version (R32–R35, FRD §3.3).**
  - *Hard floor:* never propose, plan or optimise below **1,200 kcal/day** for any adult.
  - *Soft floor by equation coefficient:* 1,200 kcal/day with the −161 coefficient and 1,500 kcal/day with the +5 coefficient (the AHA ranges).
  - *Deficit cap:* 500–750 kcal/day, whichever of that cap and the −15% default is smaller.
  - *Hard stop:* any target under 1,000 kcal/day gets the NIDDK-style response: reset the goal and offer more time.
  - *Manual and clinician targets (FR-004):* targets below the floor are stored with their source, but the planner does not optimise to them, and the app shows a "check with your clinician" note.
- **A safety screen before any weight-change plan (R34, R35, R37, R38, FR-008).** The screen holds optional SCOFF-style items plus pregnancy, breastfeeding and GLP-1 status. Two or more SCOFF "yes" answers, pregnancy or breastfeeding put the user in tracking-only mode: neutral wording, no deficit, a link to resources. A GLP-1 user gets protein-first targets and no added deficit. A "hide numbers" display option should be considered.
- **Separate, explicit, recorded consents (R2, R3, R7, R20–R22, R28, R31).** One consent each for: processing health and diary data; sending photos, voice and text to *Google* as a third-party AI, naming the provider; each HealthKit type; microphone and photos; and optional model-training or research use (R17). Store each consent's version, time and method. Withdrawing must be one tap. No paid feature may depend on any consent.
- **Keep HealthKit-derived values out of Gemini prompts (R7, R1, R8).** Apple allows HealthKit data to go to a third party only if that party provides a health service to the user. Activity and weight data stay in the deterministic engine.
- **The data-residency choice needs an ADR with counsel (R18, R19, R20, R26, R28).**
  - *Option A, one region for everyone:* Saudi health data then leaves the Kingdom. That needs an adequate destination on SDAIA's list or safeguards, plus a risk assessment for continuous sensitive transfers. Egyptian users need a cross-border licence from the PDPC.
  - *Option B, KSA users served from Dammam:* access goes through the reseller CNTXT or invoiced billing, and which Gemini models run there is unconfirmed.
  - *Either way:* Google's ML-processing residency covers only listed models and excludes Search grounding, so grounding stays off. Firebase Auth, Crashlytics and Remote Config fall outside the residency list, so they carry no health values.
- **Deletion as a single job (R4, R23, R31, FR-078).** Deletion lives in the app and needs no email or phone. It removes the account, media and backups (on the backup cycle), sends deletion requests to processors (Google), and revokes Sign in with Apple tokens. It completes within 30 days, the stricter of Saudi Arabia's 30 days and the GDPR's one month. It leaves a completion record with no identifying data, and it shows the subscription warning first.
- **App Store submission checklist (R5, R9–R15).**
  - Submit through an organisation account, not an individual one.
  - Publish a privacy policy covering retention, revoking consent and third-party AI.
  - Fill in the privacy labels: Health, Fitness, Photos, Audio, all linked to the user.
  - Ship a privacy manifest that declares the required-reason APIs, with each SDK carrying its own.
  - Choose Health & Fitness as the category and declare "not a regulated medical device" for the EEA, UK and US.
  - Build with the iOS 26 SDK now and the iOS 27 SDK from April 2027.
  - Sell the AI tier as in-app purchases. Credits must not expire, and the paywall must spell out what the user gets.
- **Wellness positioning and wording (R6, R16, R36, FRD §19.3).** No diagnosis and no promised weight loss. Never present AI output as medical advice. Show the RMR as an estimate with roughly ±10% uncertainty, and include a "check with a doctor" line where targets are set.
- **Governance work before launch, owned by the platform admin and privacy reviewer (R24, R25, R29, R30).**
  - A DPIA covering both Saudi IR Art. 25 and GDPR Art. 35.
  - A DPO appointment, since HealthKit and wearable data is SDAIA's own monitoring example.
  - A 72-hour breach runbook covering both SDAIA and the Egyptian PDPC.
  - Records of processing.
  - **Egypt:** the compliance window closes around 1 Nov 2026, so a PDPC licence for sensitive data comes before any Egyptian launch.
  - **EU:** either leave out EU storefronts at launch or appoint an Art. 27 representative.
- **AI photo estimates stay drafts (R39, R40).** Expect errors of about 35% or more, worst on large and mixed plates. Keep "AI interprets, the database resolves, the eater confirms". Show the range and evidence badge. Benchmark gemini-3.8-flash on our own weighed Egyptian and Saudi plates before rollout, because published Gemini results are poor and model-specific.
- **Where the operation is heading (R41–R43).**
  - A GLP-1 profile option: protein-first targets of 1.2–1.6 g/kg using adjusted weight, logging that suits small and frequent meals, and nutrient-adequacy hints at low intakes.
  - Voice logging is standard in competing apps, so keep it P0.
  - Adaptive targets (FR-060 and FR-061, P1) follow MacroFactor's trend-weight approach but stay bounded and need the eater's approval.
