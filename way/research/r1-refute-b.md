Refuter for research cycle 1. Checked on 2026-10-01 against `r1-platforms.md` and `r1-rules-trends.md`. I did not write those findings. Wherever a claim was in doubt, I dropped it.

Still blocked: pdpc.gov.eg (TLS failure), laws.boe.gov.sa, sciencedirect.com and bmj.com (403 or CAPTCHA), and the official text on eur-lex (empty 202).

**Verdicts.**
- `stands`: the source says it, the claim follows from it, and it is current.
- `stands (now opened)`: the finding was an assumption, or rested on a mirror or a secondary page, and I have now opened the primary source and it confirms the claim.
- `refuted (in part)`: the headline claim, or a sub-claim a decision rests on, is contradicted. The corrected statement is given.
- `doubtful`: the source exists but does not carry the claim as worded.
- `assumption (unverified)`: I found no source that settles it.

A finding whose main claim holds but that carries a small error is marked `stands` here. The error is listed under **Dropped**.

## Dropped

These claims or sub-claims must not enter the records as written:

1. **P5 / platform implication 2.** "3.8 Flash and 3.5 Flash-Lite *drop* `temperature`, `top_p` and `top_k`." Google says these parameters are *deprecated and not recommended for all Gemini 3.x models*, and they are still accepted: Google's own text-generation doc sets `temperature: 1.0` on `gemini-3.8-flash`. Keep the design conclusion: do not rely on temperature, and get repeatability from a schema, server validation and a system instruction, as Google recommends ("To ensure determinism, we recommend defining a system instruction"). Also add that `thinking_level="minimal"` **errors** on 3.8 Flash.
2. **P2.** The 3.5 Flash-Lite retirement date "July 21, 2027 or later". The official table says "No shutdown date announced".
3. **P6.** "2.5-* is legacy and deprecated". Officially the 2.5 models are "not deprecated", but they are limited to users who used them before, so they are unavailable to new projects. The 2.0 models are *shut down*, not just deprecated (June 1, 2026).
4. **P11 / platform implication 4.** "Requests stay in one region if you set the region explicitly", and the possibility of 3.8 Flash in me-central2. Endpoints "don't guarantee data residency or in-region ML processing". 3.8 Flash and 3.5 Flash-Lite are served only on `global` and the `us`/`eu` multi-regions, and no Middle East region lists any Gemini model.
5. **P23.** "IntentModes (iOS 27)". It is iOS 26.0.
6. **P41 / platform implication 10.** "`consume=True` blocks replays". On its own it does not. It returns `already_consumed`, and the server must reject a request when that key is true.
7. **R18.** Cloud Tasks as pinnable for residency. It is not on Google's data-residency list.
8. **R34.** NICE CG189 as current guidance, and "NG246 has the same very-low-calorie wording". CG189 was replaced in Jan 2025. NG246 restricts *800–1,200 kcal/day* diets to specialist services and drops the 600 kcal deficit figure.
9. **R39 (O'Hara).** Reading ICC 0.56 / 0.31 as ChatGPT's accuracy against the truth. Those are agreement figures between raters. Keep only "93% food identification; >10% error for 13 of 16 nutrients, underestimating 11".
10. **R42.** "MacroFactor starts from the Cunningham equation". MacroFactor says it uses "a set of custom equations".
11. **R29 (doubtful).** The decree type ("Prime Ministerial" against "Ministerial/MCIT") and "in force 2 Nov 2025" are unconfirmed. Record "issued 1 Nov 2025, grace period strictly to 1 Nov 2026, possibly treated as end-2026 (CMS)".
12. **Rules implication, "hard floor 1,200 kcal" attributed to sources.** No source names 1,200 as a hard floor. NIDDK's hard floor is **1,000**; AHA's *prescribing range* starts at 1,200 for women; and NG246 puts 800–1,200 kcal/day in the "low-energy diet, specialist services only" class. Taken together, these support 1,200 as a *product policy*, which the nutrition approver must own, not as a quoted rule.

## High-impact assumptions

These are still unverified, and a product or architecture decision rests on each:

1. **Saudi Arabic voice quality.** `gemini-3.5-transcribe` lists only **ar-EG**, with no ar-SA or Gulf Arabic, and on Agent Platform it is a *preview* served only from `global`. Nothing published measures Saudi dialect or Arabic–English code-switching accuracy, either for it or for 3.8 Flash audio. Voice logging is P0, so this needs our own benchmark before launch.
2. **Data residency for KSA users (new, high impact).** Because no Gemini model is offered in Dammam, every Saudi request to Gemini leaves the Kingdom, at best to the US or EU multi-region. Whether that transfer is lawful depends on SDAIA's adequacy list. Dentons reported in May 2025 that the list "has not yet been published by SDAIA"; I could not confirm its status as of Oct 2026. Without the list, the transfer needs SCCs or binding common rules plus a risk assessment (R26). This needs an ADR with counsel.
3. **Arabic Siri App Shortcut phrases (P22).** No source confirms that App Shortcut phrases work when Siri runs in Arabic. The Siri path for Arabic users is unproven.
4. **On-device Arabic speech on iOS 27 (P27).** Unverified. This matters less because the architecture already routes Arabic to the server, but it decides whether there is any offline Arabic path.
5. **Egypt PDPC licensing in practice (R28/R29).** The law requires a licence for sensitive data and a licence or permit for transfers abroad, and a foreign controller must appoint an Egyptian representative. The PDPC's licensing procedure and timelines were not opened, because pdpc.gov.eg failed TLS. This gates any Egyptian launch after about 1 Nov 2026.
